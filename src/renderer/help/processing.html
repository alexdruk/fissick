Processing

What happens during processing, how long it takes, and how to manage it

What Fossick does during processing

When you click Start Processing, Fossick works through your entire photo library in a few steps. You don't need to do anything — just let it run.

1

Extracts your files

If you dropped ZIP files, Fossick streams through them without unpacking the full archive to disk. It finds every photo, video, and sidecar file.

2

Matches sidecars to photos

For each photo, Fossick finds the companion sidecar file that contains its original date and GPS coordinates. This matching logic handles Google's filename quirks, including the 2024 format change — see the box below.

3

Writes EXIF metadata

For each matched photo, Fossick writes the correct date and GPS coordinates into the photo file itself. This is the step that makes your photos import correctly into Apple Photos, Lightroom, or any other app.

4

Builds your archive

Fossick indexes everything into a local database: Albums, Trips (grouped by location and date), and Places (photos clustered by geography).

How long will it take?

Library sizeApproximate time
Under 2,000 photos5–15 minutes
2,000–10,000 photos20–45 minutes
10,000–50,000 photos1–4 hours
50,000+ photos4–12 hours

These are estimates for a modern laptop. The main factor is the number of photos, not the total file size. Videos take a little longer per file than photos.

It's okay to leave it running

Processing continues in the background even if you minimise Fossick. Your computer can sleep between batches — Fossick will pause and resume automatically. If you're processing overnight, plug in your charger and disable sleep for the display (but not the system).

Pausing and resuming

Click the Pause button at any time to pause processing. Click Resume to continue from where you left off. No work is lost when you pause.

Click Abort to stop processing entirely. The photos that were already processed will still have their corrected EXIF data — only the unprocessed ones will remain unchanged.

If Fossick closes mid-processing

If your computer restarts or Fossick closes unexpectedly during processing, the next time you open Fossick you'll see a blue banner at the top of the screen offering to Resume from where it left off, or Discard & Start Fresh. Choosing Resume will pick up exactly where Fossick left off — no need to reprocess photos that were already fixed.

About the 2024 Google format change

In late 2024, Google quietly changed the name format for sidecar files (the companion files that store date and GPS data). They also started truncating filenames at 46 characters. This broke most other tools. Fossick uses a four-step matching strategy that handles the old format, the new format, truncated names, and edited photos — so even if your export was downloaded after the change, your photos will be matched correctly.

Trips

How Fossick groups your photos into trips and what the map shows

What are Trips?

Trips is one of the most satisfying parts of Fossick. After processing, Fossick automatically groups your GPS-tagged photos into trips — coherent journeys defined by where you went and when.

Each trip card shows a cover photo, a geocoded name (like Paris, France or Kyoto, Japan), the date range, duration, and how many photos were taken. Clicking a trip opens a full map with your exact GPS track.

How trip detection works

Fossick looks at the GPS coordinates and timestamps of all your photos. When it sees a cluster of photos in a new location, separated from your home by a significant distance, it marks that as a trip. Trips end when you return home or move on to a significantly different location.

Trips are named after the most prominent location within them, determined by reverse geocoding — looking up what city or region corresponds to those GPS coordinates. This works offline using a bundled place database, so no internet connection is needed.

Trip detection is based on your photos, not your location history

Even if you didn't export Location History from Google, Trips still works — it uses the GPS coordinates embedded in your photos. Photos taken on a phone almost always have GPS. Trips are only shown for days where at least a handful of GPS-tagged photos exist.

The trip map

Click any trip to open the full trip view. You'll see:

  • A Leaflet map centred on the trip's location, showing your GPS track as a coloured line
  • Photo pins at the location of each GPS-tagged photo — click a pin to see a thumbnail
  • A photo strip below the map showing all photos from the trip in chronological order
  • The trip name, date range, duration, and total photo count in the header

Computing trips

Trips are computed when you click the Compute Trips button in the Trips tab. This takes a minute or two. If you add new photos to your archive later, you can recompute trips to include them.

Trips only appear for GPS-tagged photos

Photos without GPS coordinates won't appear on trip maps. If most of your trips are missing from this view, it usually means your photos don't have GPS data — possibly because location was turned off on the camera or phone, or because the sidecar files weren't found. Check the Photos & Videos status tags for your library.

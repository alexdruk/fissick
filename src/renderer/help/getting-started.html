Getting Started

What Fossick does, what you need, and how to get your Google data

What Fossick does

If you've ever downloaded your photos from Google and found that they all import into Apple Photos or Lightroom with today's date — or that the location information is missing — Fossick is the tool that fixes that.

When Google exports your photos, it stores the original date and location in a separate sidecar file alongside each photo. The photo itself arrives with stripped or incorrect metadata. Fossick reads those sidecar files and writes the correct date and GPS coordinates back into the actual photo — so when you import to any photo app, everything lines up perfectly.

Beyond photos, Fossick also reconstructs your Albums, groups your photos into Trips automatically, and lets you browse your entire archive by Place (Paris, Tokyo, New York).

What you need

To use Fossick, you need a Google Takeout export of your Google Photos (and optionally, your Google location history). You don't need to be a developer. You don't need to run any commands. Just download your data from Google, point Fossick at it, and it does the rest.

Good to know

Fossick works entirely on your computer. Your photos are never uploaded anywhere. Google never sees them. No internet connection is required once you have your Takeout export.

How to request your Google Takeout export

Getting your data from Google takes a few minutes to set up, then Google emails you when it's ready (usually within a few hours, sometimes up to a few days for large libraries).

1

Go to Google Takeout

Open takeout.google.com in your browser. Sign in with the Google account whose photos you want to export.

2

Deselect everything, then select only Google Photos

Click Deselect all, then scroll down and check the box next to Google Photos. If you also want your location history (for the Trips and Places features), also check Location History.

3

Choose your export settings

Click Next step. Choose Send download link via email, Export once, and set the file size to 2 GB or 4 GB. For large libraries Google will split the export into multiple ZIP files — that's normal and Fossick handles it.

4

Wait for the email

Google will email you one or more download links. Click each link to download the ZIP files. For a large photo library, you might get 10 or more ZIPs — download all of them.

5

Open Fossick and import

Drag all the ZIP files into Fossick, or click Choose Folder if you've already extracted them. See the Import section for details.

Large libraries

If you have 10+ years of photos, your export may be 50–200 GB across many ZIP files. This is normal. Don't extract the ZIPs — just drop them all into Fossick at once. See Processing for what to expect with large libraries.

What to expect

After you start processing, Fossick works through your photos one by one. For a library of 10,000 photos, this typically takes 20–40 minutes. For 50,000 photos, expect 2–4 hours. You'll see a live progress bar and can leave it running in the background.

When it's done, your Photos & Videos, Trips, Albums, and Places sections will all be populated and ready to browse. You can then export your fixed photos to a folder, ready to import into Apple Photos, Lightroom, or any other app.
